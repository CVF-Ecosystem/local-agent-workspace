# Editing Vietnamese in context

Rely on the purpose, the readers and any voice sample already given. For business text, keep it clear,
neutral and consistent in terminology; do not try to turn every document into spoken language.

You may cut padding, replace cliched phrases with concrete actions and merge duplicate ideas. Keep real
information, figures, the degree of certainty, quotations and meaningful limits. Do not change "may" to
"will" or "planned" to "committed", and do not add details to make a sentence livelier. If a sentence
lacks a basis, point out the gap or keep the cautious wording.

Role names, terms, form codes and verbatim quotations may need to stay as they are. Do not mechanically
ban punctuation, the passive voice, tables or bullet points: choose by context.

When the request is "less machine-like", "more natural" or the text looks formulaic, you may open
`references/machine-style-signs.md` to recognise the common signs and the cases that should not be
treated as errors. If the user gives a sample of their own writing, prefer that sample.

Edit only the part you were given. Usually just return the edited version; add an explanation when a
change could affect meaning or the user wants a comparison. There is no need to produce a draft, a list
of "AI signs" and a final version for an ordinary editing request.

When the content keeps its meaning and has the desired voice, stop; do not run several "humanize"
rounds on your own or apply this skill after every document you produce.
