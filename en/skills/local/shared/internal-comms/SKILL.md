# Internal communication

Use when writing for people in the organisation who read quickly: leaders, colleagues, related
departments. Use the organisation's template and voice if there is one. Long, multi-chapter
documents: use `office-documents`. A short update that is read in a minute belongs here; a document with
many sections that will become a lasting procedure or form belongs to `office-documents`.

## General principles

- What the reader needs to know or do comes first; detail follows.
- Use only information in the sources the user supplied. Do not look for more, guess causes, or add
  commitments or deadlines.
- The bigger the department, the less detail: a team report names concrete work, a whole-unit report
  gives only the main results.
- Short sentences, figures with units and period; finished work is different from work in progress.
- Missing department name, reporting period or reader that cannot be inferred: ask once, in one message.

## Choosing the form

**Progress report (3P)** - read in 30 to 60 seconds:

- *Progress:* results achieved in the period (work done, milestones reached), not just a list of activities.
- *Plans:* priorities for the next period; give the owner and timing only when confirmed.
- *Problems:* what is slowing the work and who must help. If there are none, write "None".

**Announcement:** what changes, from when, who is affected, what the reader must do, whom to contact.

**Newsletter:** about 10 to 20 items, one or two sentences each, grouped by theme; with the document
name or path of the source if available. Use a "we/the unit" voice when it suits the organisation's culture.

**FAQ:** collect the questions many people ask; keep each answer short and based on real sources. A
question without a confirmed answer gets "awaiting confirmation"; do not answer it yourself.

**Incident report:** the sequence over time, the impact, the handling done, the current status, next
steps. Separate what is confirmed from what is suspected; do not blame individuals when the source does not.

## When delivering

Usually deliver the draft in the conversation; create a file only when asked. Check the figures,
dates and names against the source once, then stop. Do not add alternative versions or translations on your own.
