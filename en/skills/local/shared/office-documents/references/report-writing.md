# Writing style and outlines for reports and proposals

General suggestions for reports sent to a superior or a board. The unit's own rules and style always come first. Formal layout (national motto, "To:", signature block): `vn-admin-documents`.
Short updates read in one minute: `internal-comms`.

## Style

- The subject is the group ("The Executive Board reports...", "The company has carried out..."), an objective tone, no exclamations or grand adjectives; when a result is good, let the figures speak.
- **Quantify**: every judgement comes with concrete figures, a clear unit and at least one comparison point (same period last year, annual plan, target). Example: "Revenue reached 48 billion VND, down 12% from the same
  period and only 32% of the annual plan."
- **Do not hide bad news**: state difficulties, risks and shortcomings with their causes and solutions; an "all rosy" report is less convincing. End each section with something actionable: a difficulty comes with a
  solution, a proposal says to whom and what to do.
- Large sums are written in billions or millions of VND, with the exact figure in the table; thousands use the Vietnamese dot separator.
- Current figures come from the user's documents; ask when they are missing. If an assumption is unavoidable, write "assumption, to be updated with real data" right where it is used, not buried in a footnote. Figures in Word and
  the Excel appendix match exactly.
- **Shortened phrases that are easy to misread**: a phrase like "tự có" ("own") can be read as "already available, free" when talking about equipment that will be bought. Distinguish new investment from assets already owned; where
  the phrase is used to contrast with outsourcing, write it out in full. This mistake once caused a serious misunderstanding of a project's cost.

## Periodic report (half-year, year, plan)

1. Opening: "To:", basis, lead-in sentence.
2. Part A, performance: aggregate indicators (revenue, cost, profit; a table of this period against the same period and the plan, with a percentage column); assessment by business line or region; advantages and difficulties
   separated, each with figures; remedial measures tied to the responsible unit; cost, investment purchases, staff and employee income where relevant.
3. Part B, next-period plan: basis, measures to complete it, proposals and recommendations.
4. Closing and signature block.

## Investment report or proposal

1. Statement of the problem and basis (opportunity, the problem to solve, strengths, the assignment received).
2. Techno-economic analysis frame (operating requirements, capacity balance, proposed operating model, cost estimate and break-even where relevant).
3. Proposed configuration or option, item by item.
4. Total investment by item, stating that unit prices are estimates to be updated with real quotations.
5. Implementation roadmap (phases, milestones, disbursement).
6. Legal procedures needed (permits, planning, environment) if any.
7. Planned operating staff if any.
8. Risk assessment and responses (risk group, scenario, response).
9. Conclusion and recommendations.

If the proposal contains calculations (investment cost, capacity balance, unit cost, break-even), add an Excel appendix with real formulas (`excel-workbooks`) so the approver can trace how it was worked out.

## Letters, submissions, minutes

- Letters and submissions are short, usually one page: a brief reason or basis; go straight to the request, proposal or notice; a submission asking for approval says what is to be approved, by which level, and the wished-for reply
  date if any.
- Minutes and summary reports: objective narration in order, no subjective evaluation. For the structure of meeting minutes see `meeting-minutes` and `vn-admin-documents`.
